## What actually drives the price

Four variables move a project inside its range. Everything else is noise.

### 1. Page count and template architecture

A five-page site and a fifty-page site are not a 10× price difference — they share architecture — but page *templates* are. Every distinct layout (case study, article, pricing, product) is designed and engineered once as a template, then reused. More unique templates means more design and engineering hours.

### 2. Motion complexity

Standard transitions and hover states cost little. Bespoke motion — scroll choreography, kinetic typography, 3D scenes, custom cursors — is genuine engineering time, and it is the most common place budgets quietly inflate. Motion should be scoped like a feature, not improvised mid-build.

### 3. Content readiness

Copy and assets in hand keeps budgets tight. Content strategy, copywriting and art direction add real scope. Teams that underestimate content are the same teams whose timelines slip — the build waits on words more often than code.

### 4. Integrations

CMS, commerce, CRM, payments, analytics, authentication: each integration is scoped and quoted individually. A Sanity CMS is a known quantity; a custom ERP sync is not.

## What cheap quotes are hiding

A $300 "Next.js website" almost always means one of three things: a purchased template with minimal changes, an AI-generated scaffold nobody has audited, or a scope that will be renegotiated once work starts. All three cost more than doing it properly — the first two in performance and maintainability, the third in change orders.

There is a real floor below which the work cannot be done properly. A $500 landing page is honest because the scope is genuinely small — one page, one job. It is not a $4,000 build sold at a loss; it is a smaller promise, kept.

The other end matters too: a $40,000 marketing site usually means agency overhead — account managers, business development, office costs — layered onto the same engineering hours. You are paying for coordination you may not need.

## What ongoing costs look like

The build is not the only cost. Budget for:

- **Hosting** — Vercel or similar runs from free tiers to a few hundred dollars a month at scale.
- **CMS** — Sanity and Contentful have free tiers that cover most early-stage needs.
- **Maintenance** — dependency updates, security patches and performance monitoring. Monthly care plans start around $150 (₹8,000).
- **Iteration** — the highest-performing sites change constantly. Plan for improvement cycles, not a launch-and-forget build.

## How to budget correctly

Fix your scope in writing before comparing quotes: page templates, required integrations, motion expectations, content ownership. Then compare fixed-scope quotes — not hourly rates. Hourly billing punishes you for your developer's inefficiency; fixed scope rewards it.
